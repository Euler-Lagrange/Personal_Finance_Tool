# Household opening and PayPal reconciliation - November 3, 2026

## Scope

One reconciled household opening amount, received Chelsea repayments held in PayPal, separate bank cash-outs with partial/combined receipt allocations, explicit mixed-fund and fee breakdowns, manual/import forms, migration and recovery. This is an intermediate synthetic-only checkpoint. Complete bank coverage, automatic source classification, shared bills/everyday filters and exportable household statements remain in the queue.

## Financial behavior and review

- The dated signed opening amount adds only to the all-time shared balance. It creates no event, spending, income or account activity. It must precede recorded shared activity; backdated shared activity at or before it is rejected until the opening is reconciled. Confirmation, revisions, notes and audit history protect changes. Both directions and a zero correction are supported.
- A held Chelsea repayment reduces the shared balance once. Cash-outs use actual bank dates and net deposits, with receipt allocations capped at each receipt's unallocated amount. One deposit can combine receipts, and one receipt can be cashed out in parts. Cash-outs create no new settlement or income.
- Receipts plus explicitly explained other PayPal funds must equal the bank deposit plus fee. The fee uses a selected category and Jacob's budget; it is deducted inside PayPal and never subtracted from checking a second time. Other funds are existing money, with their original income/refund/transfer recorded separately if needed. The displayed receipt remainder is not claimed to be the full PayPal wallet balance.
- Allocated receipt amount/date/payer/account changes and deletion/undo are blocked. Description/notes remain editable. Cash-out edits atomically replace allocations and account effects. Deleting or undoing a cash-out frees allocations and reverses its deposit/fee while preserving the received repayment.
- Explicit imported cash-outs use the same core. Overlapping evidence can link the original deposit, and other source references protect narrow undo. A PayPal description alone never classifies a receipt. No optional PayPal export adapter is required for these workflows.
- Schema 10 keeps legacy held and direct-bank settlements unchanged; it invents neither cash-outs nor an opening balance. Legacy same-entry bank corrections remain compatible, but the interface guides new receipts into the separate cash-out workflow.

## Findings and fixes

- Added receipt-allocation protection to both normal deletion and import undo. Fee lines and reporting use the fee amount while account activity uses the net deposit; recovery validates that distinction.
- Expanded older-version test fixtures to remove the new schema-10 tables before simulating an old database. Without that fixture correction, four upgrade tests attempted to recreate tables that could not have existed in their stated source version.
- The new version-9 restore test initially selected the oldest automatic backup, which predated its direct-bank receipt. It now restores an explicitly captured version-9 fixture; preserved bank activity and receipt availability are verified.
- Forms retain inputs after validation failures, require confirmation, guard unsaved cancellation, and use non-submit cancellation/removal controls. Receipt fetches are workspace-scoped with stale-response guards.
