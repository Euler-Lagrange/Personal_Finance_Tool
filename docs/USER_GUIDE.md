# User guide

## Pay & hours

Open **Pay & hours → Pay settings** to add your hourly rate and its effective date. The editor offers $93/hour; save the date on which that rate actually began. Usual workweek defaults to eight hours Monday–Friday and remains editable. Overtime uses the same rate.

In **Weekly hours**, choose any date in the workweek, then **Edit this week**. Copying the usual week fills planned work only. Leave worked hours blank until known; enter zero for a confirmed day off. Record sick time in whole hours. Spreadsheet rows can be pasted into the editor and corrected before saving. An error keeps the draft. The gross figures are estimates before withholding and do not create a financial transaction.

In **Holidays & allowance**, enter the official PSW holiday dates for the chosen year, then review the year setup. Enter sick hours already used outside the app's records and confirm when the complete holiday list has been checked. The allowance resets to 40 each January 1. Without this setup, sick pay remains marked for review. Holidays may be worked but cannot use sick hours; any Monday–Sunday week with sick time must total at most 40 paid hours. If later work or a corrected holiday makes sick time invalid, the app preserves the actual work and flags the sick allocation instead of silently changing it.

Use **Paychecks** to add explicit pay dates and their Monday-start workweeks. Review frequency and benefits exceptions separately. In Paychecks or Holidays & allowance, browse/drop/paste payroll sources, open the saved-source list, and review proposals or enter dates from the original. Overlapping weeks show both paydays; choose an existing period under Apply as to replace it. Confirm the review before applying. DOCX embedded images and unknown layouts require comparison with the original; full years are never inferred from a filename. The calendar has month/year views. Use **Pay variations → Known paychecks** to add or edit facts, including missing take-home values, deductions and source attachments. A structured kind=pay row can prefill a draft; review before using it. **Compare hours** shows hypothetical total-hours predictions, support/range, fit error and incremental take-home, without changing timesheets. Under **Paychecks → Predict pay**, choose Manual or Known-pay model, explain assumptions, preview, then save/select a retained version. Older versions keep original inputs/results; Inputs changed signals later edits. A known fact linked to the period supplies actual-minus-predicted comparison, independently of bank matching. For **Offline withholding** or **Withholding with known-pay calibration**, first add a reviewed profile in **Pay settings**. Enter modern W-4 elections, local taxes (explicitly confirm none if appropriate), and benefit/deduction tax treatment. The prediction needs same-employer SS/Medicare YTD wages immediately before that payment; blank is unknown, including cap usage. Future YTD must include expected intervening pay. Tax rules currently support 2026 only. **Known paychecks → Compare withholding** stores historical YTD context and modeled-versus-actual net. At least two compatible earlier comparisons support calibration, with a visible correction limit and supporting history. Editing an input clears the preview; saving keeps original inputs and creates no bank income. Use **Pay & deposits** to approve a saved estimate and split its expected net across bank accounts. Review any generic paycheck schedule for takeover before allowing a second forecast. Match existing ordinary income deposits explicitly; partial deposits and one combined deposit across periods are supported. Mark reconciliation complete when all deposits arrived, even if actual net differs. Unlink before changing or deleting linked financial records. **Review timesheet** requires recorded actual work (explicit zero for unpaid usual days); later edits show Needs review. **Annual payroll** separates worked gross, approved expectations and allocated bank pay. Month close saves its original payroll explanation. Automatic destination backups are available in **Workspace & backups → Folder backups**.

Hover over a small circled **i** beside a page title or section for an explanation of what it does. You can also Tab to the icon or click it. Move the pointer into the explanation to keep reading; press Escape or click elsewhere to dismiss it. Escape closes the help first if you are editing a transaction, preserving the form. Page descriptions give a short starting point, while help explains distinctions such as budget room versus checking cash, bills versus payments, and planned versus recorded activity.

A Windows desktop app for monthly budgeting, bills, savings goals, and shared expenses between Jacob and Chelsea. The core workflow works offline; optional Claude and OpenAI requests can produce reviewed proposals for difficult imports.

The initial build is implemented with Tauri 2, React/TypeScript, Rust, and SQLite. It covers manual entry, editable accounts and categories, monthly limits, the Jacob/Chelsea shared balance, transfers and card payments, reviewed imports, merchant rules, refunds, bills, savings goals, month close and annual reporting, cash forecasts, PDF templates, optional reviewed AI, local recovery and encrypted laptop handoff. The approved initial build queue is complete and ready for Jacob's live-data trial; external acceptance remains unverified.

## Local data and recovery

The native core stores `demo.sqlite` and `personal.sqlite` in the current Windows user's local app-data directory under `com.jacob.personal-finance`; the app shows the exact path in **Workspace & backups**. Databases are outside the source checkout. Rebuilding the program does not reset them.

Recovery snapshots use SQLite's backup API before financial mutations, retain the newest 20 copies per workspace, and validate integrity, schema version, workspace identity, references, and source-evidence hashes before restore. Restoring also backs up the active state first. Import evidence is stored inside SQLite in this preview, so backups include originals and review state; snapshots can grow with the evidence library. Limits are 10 MB per source, 5,000 rows / 80 columns, 25 waiting files, and 250 MB of source bytes per workspace. These local copies are not encrypted portable archives. Data relies on the user's Windows account and device protection; the app does not assume BitLocker is enabled.

Accounts show recorded activity and an estimate from the latest confirmed end-of-day balance when one exists. Starting accounts/categories are editable; Personal has sheet-based category defaults but no saved monthly limits until you set or copy them. Bank connections remain outside the current offline workflow; statement review and category rollover are available. Savings contributions and withdrawals between your accounts use **Bank transfer**, not income/spending. A credit card's negative activity can reflect payments of charges that have not been recorded here; it is not a claim that the issuer owes you money.

Schema version 43 upgrades older databases after a recovery backup. It includes the category preset, AI usage, payroll and managed-restore protections while preserving customized setup, saved monthly limits, financial records, sources, review decisions, profiles and receipts. Earlier migrations retain source identities/coverage and separate shared evidence bytes from each download observation without inventing new coverage. Older recovery snapshots are validated and migrated in isolation before replacing live data. Recovery checks account effects, allocations, source evidence, alias provenance, coverage compatibility and operation dependencies. Financial changes are audited, transactional and revision-checked. Import operations have retry receipts; rules recheck reviewed versions and source identity.

## Encrypted folder backups

Open **Workspace & backups → Folder backups → Set up folder**. Browse to an existing local or OneDrive folder, choose a recovery passphrase of at least 12 characters, repeat it, and confirm that you have kept it independently. Remembering it in Windows Credential Manager enables automatic copies while the app is open. This does not add an app-login password. The first copy is encrypted, reopened and validated before retention can run. A changed destination receives a new owned namespace; previous folders remain untouched. Existing sets keep their original passphrase.

Automatic backups coalesce changes for about 10 seconds, at most 60 seconds of continuous edits, and run on a five-second worker tick. Completed imports and month close expedite a checkpoint; launch checks for changes since the last copy. The financial save completes independently of backup failure. Use **Save folder backup** to retry or save a protected checkpoint. Review last-success/errors here; **Pause automatic backups** leaves existing files available. No backups run after the app exits, and pending edits catch up on the next launch.

Default retention keeps the latest copy from 30 distinct days and 12 distinct months, plus the newest verified archive and any protected checkpoints. Monthly means the latest available snapshot in that month, not a promise that the app ran on the last calendar day. The app only rotates its own cataloged files in this device namespace. Unknown files, interrupted partials, other-device files and previous-destination files are not deleted. If an older file changed or cannot be verified, retention stops with an explanation while the newly verified copy remains saved.

**Backup saved to selected folder** confirms the local file only. **Cloud sync not verified** means you should check OneDrive independently. Make required archives available on this device for offline recovery. Folder backups are not automatic laptop synchronization; continue using reviewed handoff when moving work between devices.

To restore, select **Review restore** on a saved copy or **Restore an encrypted backup** and choose any intact `.pfbackup`. Enter its original passphrase, preview the workspace/date/counts, then explicitly confirm replacement. The current local branch is preserved as a `.managed.sqlite` recovery point and excluded from ordinary rotation. No automatic merge occurs. Restoration works without the original local catalog or Windows credential; keep your independent passphrase. Archive limits remain 256 MB of database content (258 MB encrypted file). Wrong passwords, damaged archives, stale previews and workspace mismatches leave current records unchanged.
